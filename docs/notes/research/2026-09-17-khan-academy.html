Comeni Code · research report · 17 September 2026

Be the tutor Khan Academy never could be

Khan Academy is the largest body of organised, free learning material in one place. We can't out-write it, and we shouldn't try. This report sets out what it does best, where it is weak, and how Comeni Code can sit on top of it and similar sources: it finds the order, diagnoses the gaps and explains why each step is there.

A ninth proposal was added: AI block scores, with automatic redrafts and triage, and automatic deployment deferred. Skeletons are drafted only from public or CC BY outlines; Khan Academy is used by people, and embedded and linked. Learner chat and exams are deferred past v1. AP-level prose is accepted.

The new goal

Comeni Code organises the best existing explanations (Khan Academy, OpenStax, Galaxy Training, the Carpentries and our own nodes) into the right order for your goal. It checks what you already know, takes you back a step when you stumble, and ends at something you can run.

Google didn't write the web; it organised it and won on how it presented the result. Code's equivalent is the prerequisite graph, the woven route, and the checks and reviews along it. That's the tutor's job, done by a system anyone can inspect rather than by a chatbot.

Khan Academy, briefly

Only facts that matter for our design. Its course pages are rendered by JavaScript and its help centre blocks automated readers, so page structure below comes from its help articles, blog and search results rather than from reading the pages directly.

Philosophy: mastery learningMove on only once a concept is understood, with as many tries as needed to reach 90%+. The idea comes from Bloom's 2-sigma study; the book is The One World Schoolhouse (2012).
Anatomy: course → unit → lessonLessons mix videos, articles and practice exercises, followed by quizzes, a unit test and a course challenge. AP Biology follows the College Board's 8 units, from Chemistry of Life to Ecology.
Mastery levelsAttempted → Familiar → Proficient → Mastered, worth 100 points per skill. Levels can drop. Mastery Challenges (6 questions from 3 skills, at most every 12 h) provide spaced, mixed review.
Help inside practiceA hint for each step, a rationale after answering, and hand-picked related videos on exercises.
Prerequisites, packaged by hand"Get ready for …" courses: a course challenge finds the gaps, and a review course fills them. The graph-shaped Knowledge Map was removed in 2013.
Khanmigo, the AI tutorSocratic and grounded in the learner's record. Free for teachers; $4 a month for learners and parents.
Motivation layerEnergy points, badges, avatars, and, since 2025, streaks and levels.
ProductionA content team of about 20, AI-assisted drafting with human review, atomic publishing, locale-pinned versions and the Wonder Blocks design system.

What to take

take These work, and each has a place in Code.

Mastery as visible, decaying levels

A learner sees how solid each skill is, not just whether it was done. Levels drop when answers slip.

KA Help: mastery levels; Mastery Challenges

In Code

Replace the binary "settled" with a small set of states that review can lower, with no points attached (proposal C4).

Short, informal worked-example videos

Videos under 6 minutes keep attention, and Khan-style tablet drawing engaged viewers more than polished studio lectures (6.9 M edX sessions).

Guo, Kim & Rubin, L@S 2014

In Code

Video becomes a first-class way to learn on a node: the best existing video, linked or embedded with its timestamp, and later our own short screencasts with transcripts (C5).

Hints for each step, rationale afterwards

Help arrives in two layers: while the learner is stuck, and after they answer.

In Code

try and problem blocks gain ordered hints and a rationale, reviewed like any other block (C6).

Spaced, mixed review

Mastery Challenges mix skills and return on a schedule.

Test out, then skip

A course challenge lets someone who already knows the material move ahead quickly.

One consistent anatomy for every course

Every course looks the same, so a learner knows where they are in any of them.

In Code

The node's fixed blocks already do this. Add a fixed Learn it section: our explanation first, then the best outside resources.

It measures learning, not activity

Khanmigo is tuned on next-item correctness. Surfacing unmastered prerequisites raised it by 2.7%, and adding recent history by 3.4%.

KA blog: building a better AI tutor

In Code

This is outside evidence that pointing at prerequisites helps, which is our core idea. Adopt next-item correctness after a step back as our main measure (C8).

Publishing discipline

Content is immutable once published and the site switches versions atomically; translations are pinned to a version.

KA blog: evolving our content infrastructure

In Code

We already have this through git: landed files, an index rebuilt from a commit, and tagged snapshots.

Where it is weak

weak spot Each comes with its evidence and how Code turns it into an advantage.

No way to ask "what do I need first?"

Courses are linear. Prerequisites exist only as hand-built "Get ready for" courses, mostly for maths, and the knowledge map was removed in 2013.

KA Help Center community; KA blog: Get ready for

Code's answer

The needs graph and the weaver: any goal becomes a route, with the reason each stop is there. This is our core.

Slow, hand-curated, small team

About 20 content staff, who say they can't reply to error reports. High-school biology was deprecated, and college-level work gets less priority than K-12.

KA Help: content principles; reporting mistakes

Code's answer

Code doesn't write everything: nodes link to the best existing material. AI drafts skeletons and people review them (C1, C2).

Stops at AP and intro college, never reaches doing

No bioinformatics or computational biology; a 2018 learner request for it went nowhere. Nothing connects the knowledge to real tools or data.

KA Help Center request (2018)

Code's answer

Routes from AP-level biology down to a pipeline you can run in Labs. Nobody covers that bridge (C7).

Self-directed learners drift

Only about 9% of students reach the recommended 30 minutes a week. Partner-district students are 6× more likely to, because teachers carry the structure.

KA efficacy results, Nov 2024; KA Districts

Code's answer

The route, next up and why this is here do the structuring a teacher would. We keep refusing streaks as a substitute.

Tutoring is limited and sits behind a paywall or a teacher

Khanmigo costs individual learners money. A two-year randomised trial found +1.26 percentile ranks per term, and an undergraduate physics study found no difference from using Google.

J-PAL; JTL 2025; WINS summary

Code's answer

A free, deterministic tutor: diagnose, sequence, explain, check, step back, review. No chat needed.

No sources behind claims

Videos and articles don't cite where their statements come from. Physics-education critics point to examples likely to introduce misconceptions.

Noschese 2011; Bates 2012

Code's answer

Every sentence says where it came from (our claim), and misconception callouts are required.

Gamification creep

Energy points and badges, then streaks and levels in 2025.

KA blog: streaks and levels

Code's answer

Keep invariant 10. Levels that describe what you know are fine; levels you earn as a prize are not.

Closed to builders

The public API was shut in 2020. The terms forbid scraping and using content to build AI, and videos are no longer openly licensed.

KA Help: API removal; terms summary

Code's answer

We link, never copy. Our content is CC BY and open, which is itself something to offer (see the boundaries section).

The tutor loop

What a good human tutor does, done by the system. Each station already exists in the specs, or is added by one of the proposals below.

What needs to change

proposal Each becomes spec text once you agree; where names the section it edits.

C1

Nodes point outward: a resource block

A node carries our claim, explanation and checks, plus a curated Learn it elsewhere list. Each entry records its kind (video, reading, tutorial, exercise), provider, URL, the part it covers (a timestamp range or section), its licence, what it's good for, and who reviewed it. Content CI checks that the links resolve. A person picks the entry; a model may suggest one, labelled not yet reviewed.

where: W5.1 block table · L5 Node · S3 workbench gains a Resources tab

C2

Tracks start from skeletons, not blank pages

A skeleton is a set of node stubs (a claim and a title) plus draft needs links, taken from a public framework's organisation: the College Board AP Biology course description, the OpenStax Biology 2e contents (CC BY), Galaxy Training topics (CC BY) and Carpentries lessons (CC BY). A person may read Khan Academy's course layout as a reference and cite it; nothing is extracted from it automatically. The AI drafts the stubs, the request queue receives them, and people accept them (invariant 4 holds).

where: new spec section on skeletons · W3.6 requests · R4 M1 fixtures and M7

C3

Step back, don't just correct

A wrong answer that matches a misconception shows its callout and, where the misconception points at a needs link, offers "Revisit X first" as a detour the route shows. Returning brings the learner back to the same question.

where: W5.1 callout gains an optional link to what it needs · L4 Route shows detours · L5

C4

Mastery states instead of "settled"

Four states: new · seen · checked · solid. Review can move a node down, and the weaver treats solid as known. No points, and no state is shown as a prize.

where: W3.3 learner state · L9 Your knowledge · L7 Review · W13 question 2 answered

C5

Video is a first-class way to learn

A node can lead with a video: an external one through C1 (Khan Academy's YouTube embed, used non-commercially and with attribution), or later our own screencasts under 6 minutes with required transcripts. A learner can switch between reading and watching.

where: W5.1 · L5 · W10 identity for the player frame

C6

Hints and rationales in every check

try and problem gain ordered hints (optional, one step at a time) and a rationale shown after answering. They're reviewed like prose.

where: W5.1 · §5.1.1 · S5 Problem builder

C7

Aim the slice at the bridge

The v1 demo stays learn Salmon, but the route now starts at AP-level biology nodes built from skeletons and ends at a runnable Labs pipeline. The claim to test is that someone who knows AP Biology, or less, gets there.

where: W12 slice · R4 M9 done-when

C8

Measure the tutor

The main measure is correctness on the next attempt after a step back. Secondary measures: route completion, time to a solid state, and resources marked unhelpful. These are aggregates only (invariant 9), and none is shown to learners as a score.

where: S11 Quality · W9 usage pages

Using others' work: the boundaries

allowed

  • Linking to any Khan Academy page, video or exercise.
  • Embedding Khan Academy's YouTube videos non-commercially, with attribution.
  • A person reading Khan Academy's course layout as a reference, and citing it.
  • Adapting and quoting OpenStax, Galaxy Training and Carpentries text (CC BY 4.0), with attribution. This is compatible with our CC BY 4.0.
  • Using the College Board's public AP unit and topic list as a checklist of what to cover.
  • Reading Perseus and Wonder Blocks (MIT) for ideas. No code is copied (our rule mirrors the one with Labs).

not allowed

  • Scraping or bulk-downloading Khan Academy; its terms forbid it, and the API has been gone since 2020.
  • Feeding Khan Academy content to a model to draft ours; the terms forbid using it to build AI.
  • Copying Khan Academy text or exercises into our CC BY repository. Theirs is non-commercial share-alike, which is incompatible, and the videos aren't openly licensed at all.
  • Using Khan Academy's name or logo in a way that implies a partnership.

Consequence: in C2, a model may draft skeletons only from the CC BY and public frameworks. Khan Academy's organisation enters through a person, as a cited reference. The terms page itself couldn't be fetched (it blocks automated readers), so read it once before C1 and C2 become a spec.

Effect on the roadmap

  1. M0 continues unchanged. Parts 3–9 are infrastructure, and nothing here touches them.
  2. A new spec, "Code as tutor", records C1–C8 with the rejected alternatives (compete head-on; copy content; a chat tutor). The weaving and architecture specs are edited only to point at it.
  3. M1 grows. The node schema gains resource, hints, rationales and mastery states, and the fixtures come from a small skeleton.
  4. A skeleton part (in M1 or M7): framework outline → node stubs → request queue → people accept.
  5. M3 and M9 done-when gain the video and resources on the Node page, and the AP-level start of the Salmon route.

The new finish line (M9)

A learner who knows AP Biology, or less, types learn Salmon. Code:

  1. places them and weaves a route from their gaps;
  2. at each stop, gives our checked explanation and the best existing video or reading, with sources;
  3. takes them back a step when an answer shows a gap;
  4. brings nodes back for spaced review until they're solid;
  5. ends at a Labs pipeline they can run.

Built from skeletons plus review, not 60 pages written from scratch.

Open questions

  • Learner chat (invariant 8). Khanmigo's measured effects are modest, and our tutor needs no chat. Keep the ban, or allow a narrow "explain this step" call, cached and reviewed?
  • Embed or link only? Embedding keeps learners on the page but ties us to YouTube. Linking is simpler and never breaks licence terms.
  • Which frameworks seed skeletons first? Proposed: OpenStax Biology 2e and the AP Biology course description for the biology base, and Galaxy Training for the bioinformatics end.
  • Names for the mastery states, and whether seen counts toward the route at all.
  • Audience. The specs say university students and researchers. Starting routes at AP level widens that; is AP-level prose at the start of a route acceptable?
