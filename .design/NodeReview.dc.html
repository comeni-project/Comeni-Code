Exposition — draft 2
9 assertions · 3 with no source

When you hand a tube to the sequencing facility, you are handing over millions of molecules, and what comes back is not those molecules — it is a few hundred letters read off some of them1. Each read is one observation, drawn from a population you never see directly.

This is why coverage is spoken of as depth: sequencing the same library twice gives you two different samples of the same underlying pool2. Two reads that look identical may come from one molecule read twice, or from two molecules that happen to share a sequence — and nothing in the FASTQ tells you which.

The consequence runs through everything downstream. A count is not a measurement of how much RNA was present; it is a count of how many times a sampling process happened to draw from that transcript3. That is the whole reason differential expression needs statistics rather than subtraction.

Once you see reads as samples, the rest of the pipeline stops looking like a series of file conversions and starts looking like what it is: an attempt to say something about a population from a sample of it4.
