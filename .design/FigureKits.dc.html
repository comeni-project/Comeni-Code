Choosing k

IntermediateAbout 15 min with the questions

Counting k-mers

Small k makes every k-mer common, so the graph branches everywhere; large k makes k-mers unique, but each read yields fewer of them. The table counts both for one small genome.

As k grows

In a genome without long repeats the count levels off once k-mers are unique. Repeats keep it climbing: a k-mer inside a repeat stays shared until k is longer than the repeat.

Where it fits

Salmon chooses k once, when it builds the index; every read is then cut into k-mers of the same length and looked up.

When a figure fails

The browser checks a figure’s data again before drawing it. If the check fails, nothing is half-drawn: the alt text stands in, and the caption stays.
